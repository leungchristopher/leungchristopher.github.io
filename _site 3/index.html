My name is Chris. Most of the things I do are driven by an interest in systems that learn to learn. I am currently involved in a new venture focused on reasoning.

I studied Natural Sciences at the University of Cambridge from 2023 to 2026, obtaining a double First, where I placed second in my cohort and received the Holgate-Pollard and J.M. Thoday prizes. During this time, I did research in mathematical and computational genetics at the University and the Cancer Research UK Cambridge Institute, and neuroscience at Caltech.

In a past life at Winchester, I was a finalist in the selection of the UK's teams for the International Chemistry Olympiad and International Astronomy and Astrophysics Olympiad. I also received distinctions/top golds in the British Mathematical and Physics Olympiads, and the Freeman Dyson Prize in Physics. My team and I also won the National Cipher Challenge in 2022 with a genetic algorithm.

Outside of work, I'm interested in the following problems:

  • The impact of rapidly-evolving technology on educational outcomes, and our current inability to understand these changes due to the long horizons involved;
  • Explanations of cell-type specificity in genetic diseases;
  • The role of preprocessing in analyses of biological data (think cell atlases, neural imaging, Mendelian randomisation, GWAS).
