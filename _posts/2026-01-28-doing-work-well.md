Recently I started my Part II project at the Department of Genetics in Cambridge. The plan is to construct and analyse ancestral recombination graphs constructed from viral genomes.

Eight weeks is not a lot of time. How will I make sure that the project succeeds?

In short, I don't think there's an ironclad guarantee that any research project works. Research taste can be honed, but problems will arise anyway. Pipelines break, assumptions fail, deeper truths emerge. All I can do is do the best focused, deep work possible, take good advice from my advisors and mentors, and think my way through the fog of uncertainty.

How will I try to maximise my chances of success then? And what is a good metric for 'success'?

1. Accountability. I'm keeping track of my hours on my [accountability page]. The aim is to work 25 hours per week.
2. Organisation. I'm using [Git] to track my work and [Obsidian] to keep track of tasks and meetings. Annotations and notes on papers are kept in [Zotero].
3. Seeking advice. I will always have a more precise account of the work I've done than my supervisor, because I'm the one doing it. So seek advice with that in mind.

I'll try to write something about genetics as frequently as I can: writing clears the mind.
