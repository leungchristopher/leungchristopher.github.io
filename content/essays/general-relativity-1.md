I feel like I need a mental challenge: one that really pushes the limits of how I can reason, visualise, and understand a topic. The mountain I've decided to climb is general relativity.

Why general relativity? It's arguably the pinnacle of 20th century physics, a combination of geometric ingenuity and physical insight. Moreover, I have a feeling that differential geometry and a good understanding of tensors will serve me in good stead. Personally, I came across the Friedmann equations in cosmology whilst competing in olympiads, and have captivated me ever since.

The plan is to get through [Ulrich Sperhake's Part II general relativity course](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/us248/Lectures/Notes/grII.pdf) and the corresponding [examples sheets](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/examples/) before returning to the UK.

The scope of the course is:

- Special relativity and Newtonian gravity
- Differential geometry; manifolds, tensors, geodesics, covariant derivatives, the Levi-Civita connection, parallel transport, normal coordinates, and the Riemann tensor.
- Physical laws in curved spacetimes
- The Schwarzschild solution and other tests of GR
- Cosmology: Friedmann, cosmological redshift, cosmological models and selected solutions
- Singularities and geodesic incompleteness
- Linearised theory and gravitational waves

The goal is to be able to understand $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ in its full glory. I will periodically document my progress...
