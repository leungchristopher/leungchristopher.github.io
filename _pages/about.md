I am a third-year Natural Sciences student at the University of Cambridge, based in the Department of Genetics. My interests lie at the intersection of machine learning and genetics, concerning how we navigate data domains which are spatiotemporally sparse, heterogeneous, and unreliable. This is the focus of my thesis project.

I am currently a Research Fellow of the Supervised Program for Alignment Research, with a particular interest in the limitations of behavioural evaluations. In the past, I have worked on applying deep learning to genomics at the Cancer Research UK Cambridge Institute; structural biology and biochemistry at the Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences and Weizmann Institute of Science; and liquid biopsy at the Centre for Novostics.

At school, I maintained an interest in competitive mathematics and science competitions, reaching the final stage of the selection processes for the International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics and International Chemistry Olympiad for the UK. My team also won the IBM Hut 7 Prize at the National Cipher Challenge with an evolutionary program, and I took part in the Oxford Quantum Club outreach programme.

Some of my ongoing projects include building [BetaAbalone] - my attempt to understand the AlphaZero system, and [OrcaFly], a convoultional neural network for genomics predictions. Along the way, I've used Python, C(++), modern machine learning libraries (JAX, PyTorch, TensorFlow), and gained experience with high performance computing systems. In my free time, I enjoy bouldering, tennis, and playing chamber music.
