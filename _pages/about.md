Quietly building CHCL, leveraging insights from pathogen evolution to better understand preventative measures.

I am a third-year Natural Sciences student at the University of Cambridge, based in the Department of Genetics. My interests lie at the intersection of machine learning and genetics, concerning how we navigate data domains which are spatiotemporally sparse, heterogeneous, and unreliable. This is the focus of my thesis project.

I am currently a Research Fellow of the Supervised Program for Alignment Research, with a particular interest in the limitations of behavioural evaluations. In the past, I have worked on applying deep learning to genomics at the Cancer Research UK Cambridge Institute; structural biology and biochemistry at the Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences and Weizmann Institute of Science; and liquid biopsy at the Centre for Novostics.

At school, I maintained an interest in competitive mathematics and science competitions. I was a finalist in the selection for the UK's teams at the International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics and International Chemistry Olympiad. My team also won the IBM Hut 7 Prize at the National Cipher Challenge with an evolutionary program, and I took part in the Oxford Quantum Club outreach programme.

Some of my ongoing projects include building [BetaAbalone] and [BetaConnect] - my attempts to understand deep reinforcement learning and Monte Carlo tree search; [OrcaFly], a convoultional neural network for predicting 3-dimensional genome organisation; and [ARG-RL], which will explore the use of reinforcement learning for inferring the ancestral history of DNA viruses.

Along the way, I've used Python, C(++), modern machine learning libraries (JAX, PyTorch, TensorFlow), and gained experience with high performance computing systems. In my free time, I enjoy bouldering, tennis, and playing chamber music.
